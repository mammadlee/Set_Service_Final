# SET Service — App Store privacy evidence and draft answers

Status: engineering draft for the `1.0.0+4` mobile candidate, **not** an App Store Connect submission, legal approval, or claim that an iOS archive passed Apple's checks. The same Flutter app serves Worker, Company and Admin roles. Product/legal owners must approve the final answers after reviewing the signed iOS archive, Xcode privacy report, production processors, retention schedule and actual App Store Connect configuration.

Apple's [App Privacy taxonomy](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/app-privacy-details/) defines collection as transmitting data off-device for access beyond a real-time request. Data used only for app functionality still needs disclosure. The label covers the app **and third-party SDKs**, and role-conditional data must be included unless Apple's narrow optional-disclosure criteria all apply. An app privacy label, bundled `PrivacyInfo.xcprivacy`, public privacy policy and runtime permissions are related but distinct declarations; one cannot substitute for another.

## Proposed App Privacy data inventory

“Linked” is the engineering presumption because these records are tied to accounts, orders or devices. “App Functionality” is the evidenced purpose, not a claim that no other purpose exists in production. Do not mark a field “Not Collected” merely because it is optional, belongs to one role, or is held in private storage.

Email is role/flow dependent. | Collected, linked, App Functionality. Validate processor uses and whether any secondary marketing purpose exists. |

Push permission is optional. |

Private worker health/criminal documents are delivered only after authorization and short-lived download signing, not via the public profile-photo route. Health certificates may be visible to an authorized approved company with an eligible assignment relationship; criminal-record documents are not Company-visible in the checked access rules. Profile photos have a constrained public delivery route. See `docs/google-play-data-safety.md` and the authorization regression tests for the implementation evidence. Sharing with a service provider or an authorized counterpart is **not automatically equivalent** to Apple's “Tracking”; classify actual purposes and data recipients separately.

## SDK and privacy-manifest review

The Flutter manifest includes `firebase_core`, `firebase_messaging`, `file_picker`, `mobile_scanner`, `flutter_secure_storage`, `url_launcher`, `dio` and `provider`. Apple's [third-party SDK list](https://developer.apple.com/support/third-party-SDK-requirements/) explicitly names Flutter, file_picker, FirebaseCore/FirebaseMessaging and url_launcher-related SDKs, among others. Listed SDKs need their own privacy manifests; listed binary SDKs also need signatures. Transitive iOS pods and their manifests cannot be established from `pubspec.yaml` alone. On macOS, run CocoaPods resolution with the locked dependency set, archive with the submission Xcode toolchain, inspect the assembled frameworks and generate Xcode's privacy report. Resolve missing manifests/signature warnings in the owning SDK or by updating a verified dependency; do not add fictitious approved-reason API codes to Runner to cover a third-party SDK.

Runner's current `apps/worker_app/ios/Runner/PrivacyInfo.xcprivacy` is source evidence only. It declares no tracking and names linked contact identifiers, profile photos, other content, sensitive information, Health and device identifier. The Health entry must be reconciled with actual certificate content and App Store Connect answers. Its empty `NSPrivacyAccessedAPITypes` is **not proof** that the compiled app and pods use no [required-reason APIs](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/bundleresources/describing-use-of-required-reason-api). Every required reason must reflect actual use and be declared in the owning app or SDK bundle.

## Public policy, deletion and human sign-off

Apple requires a public [privacy policy URL and App Privacy responses](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-app-information/manage-app-privacy). [Review Guideline 5.1.1](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/) also requires an easily accessible in-app policy link and, for an app with account creation, an in-app way to initiate [account deletion](https://developer.apple.com/support/offering-account-deletion-in-your-app). This repository has Worker and Company deletion actions, plus a public ownership-verification request path. Verify both roles' iOS navigation, reauthentication, actual backend completion, object cleanup and retained audit exceptions in an approved staging environment; a success toast alone is insufficient. The checked legal policy is served from `/privacy`; verify the deployed HTTPS URL and cross-platform wording before entering it in App Store Connect.

Do not publish a final label until the responsible human has documented:

1. Production SDK/privacy report, push configuration, diagnostics, IP logging, processors and subprocessors.
2. Exact Health/Sensitive/Other User Content and pay-rate classification using real collected fields.
3. Each selected data type's purposes, whether it is linked to the user, and whether tracking occurs.
4. Public policy and in-app link availability, approved retention/deletion schedule and support contact.
5. App Store Connect responses matching the archived binary, including any role-conditional collection.

No App Store Connect privacy response or iOS archive has been submitted by this document.
